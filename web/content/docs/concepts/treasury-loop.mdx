Maat is designed to pay its own way: keep a small basket of tokenized stocks, put idle stock to work as collateral, borrow USDC against it to cover its running costs, and pay its own compute bill from the vault. This page shows how the agent is built to do that, which rule decides each step, and which part is still only planned. It describes the agent's code and what the fork run covers. The agent has sent transactions only on a fork, and nothing is deployed.

## Five steps

The loop has five steps: earn, buy, supply, borrow and pay. The diagram shows the four that the agent is built to run. The first, earning, is planned and not built (see [Planned: earning](#planned-earning)).

<TreasuryLoop />

Each step is a rule in the agent's policy, `agent/src/policy.ts`, and an action the vault checks:

| Step | What the agent is built to do | Vault action | What the vault checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buy | Keep an equal-weight basket and rebalance when a weight drifts | `BUY`, `SELL` | [The nine checks](/docs/concepts/nine-checks) |
| Supply | Put idle stock into [Aave](/docs/help/glossary#aave-v4) as collateral | `SUPPLY`, and `WITHDRAW_COLLATERAL` before a sale | [The credit checks](/docs/concepts/credit-and-health-factor) |
| Borrow | Keep a runway of compute cost in USDC | `BORROW` | An open market, every supplied stock allowed and unpaused with a valid, fresh [feed](/docs/help/glossary#chainlink-equity-feed) answer and a stable [multiplier](/docs/help/glossary#uimultiplier), and a [health factor](/docs/help/glossary#health-factor) at or above the floor, or at or above the target inside the [de-risk window](/docs/help/glossary#de-risk-window) |
| Pay | Pay the unpaid compute bill | `PAY_EXPENSE` | The payee allowlist and the caps |

The policy only suggests. Claude can adopt, resize or drop a suggestion, the agent previews every intent, and the vault decides on each one ([How a decision is made](/docs/get-started/how-a-decision-is-made)). The agent starts in [dry-run](/docs/help/glossary#dry-run) mode, which is the default. In dry run it reads the chain, asks Claude and previews every intent, and it sends nothing. [Live mode](/docs/help/glossary#live-mode) needs a registered Builder Code and the [CDP Server Wallet](/docs/help/glossary#cdp-server-wallet), and neither has been used for real. The rest of this page describes what the agent is built to do when it does send.

<Callout type="fork" title="What the fork run covers">

In the agent's fork run, at block <Fact name="forkBlock" />, one round buys, supplies and borrows, and each preview matches the vault's `Decision`. A scripted proposer stands in for Claude, and a local sender stands in for the CDP wallet. Because the proposals are scripted, the run does not test whether the policy's suggestions lead to those trades, and it includes no bill payment. The bill logic is covered by the agent's unit tests with a fake chain, and the vault's `PAY_EXPENSE` path by the vault's own tests.

</Callout>

## The basket

The policy keeps an equal-weight basket of the vault's stocks that it knows by name: NVDAc, GOOGLc and AAPLc. Net equity is the vault's USDC plus the value of every stock the vault lists that has a feed price, both in the vault and supplied to Aave, minus any debt. A listed stock the agent does not know by name still counts in net equity, but it gets no target and no suggestion. The policy's defaults sit in one line of code:

```typescript title="agent/src/policy.ts#L6-L13"
export interface PolicyParams {
  cashReserveBps: number;
  driftBps: number;
  runwayDays: number;
  minTradeUsd6: bigint;
}

export const DEFAULT_POLICY: PolicyParams = { cashReserveBps: 3000, driftBps: 500, runwayDays: 7, minTradeUsd6: 1_000_000n };
```

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash reserve | <Fact name="cashReservePct" /> | The share of net equity the stocks leave in USDC |
| Target weight | <Fact name="targetWeightPct" /> | Each stock's share of net equity: the rest, split equally |
| Rebalance drift | <Fact name="rebalanceDriftPct" /> | How far a weight may sit from its target before the policy acts |
| Runway | <Fact name="runwayDays" /> | Days of compute cost kept as USDC |
| Minimum trade | <Fact name="minTradeUsd" /> | A suggestion smaller than this is dropped |

The cash reserve is not a separate rule. It is what is left when the stocks sit at their targets. A stock's weight is its value, in the vault and supplied, as a share of net equity. The policy then looks at each stock:

- **Overweight.** The weight is more than the drift above its target. The policy suggests a `SELL` of the excess, capped by the mandate's per-trade cap. If the vault does not hold enough of the stock outside Aave, and some of it is supplied and the credit position can be read, it suggests a `WITHDRAW_COLLATERAL` instead, so that the stock is back in the vault before it is sold. Otherwise the policy suggests nothing for that stock.
- **Underweight.** The weight is more than the drift below its target. The policy suggests a `BUY` of the shortfall, capped by the per-trade cap and by spendable cash.
- **Idle stock.** Stock in the vault that is not overweight gets a `SUPPLY` suggestion, when the credit position can be read.

Only a stock that the vault allows and prices is bought or supplied.

The vault's own limit on one stock, <Fact name="mandate.maxWeightBps" />, is a separate and looser check ([The mandate](/docs/concepts/mandate#maxweightbps)). The policy's targets are what the agent aims for, and the vault's cap bounds whatever is proposed. The policy tests pin these rules, including the order of suggestions: `REPAY`, `SUPPLY`, `WITHDRAW_COLLATERAL` and `SELL`, `BUY`, `BORROW` (`agent/test/policy.test.ts`).

### Worked example: an all-cash vault

The policy tests start from a vault that holds 100 USDC, no stock and no debt, with no unpaid bill and a daily compute cost that the test sets to the default, <Fact name="dailyComputeUsd" />. The figures below are the test's own numbers:

- The [runway](/docs/help/glossary#runway) is <Fact name="runwayDays" /> at <Fact name="dailyComputeUsd" /> a day, which is $3.50, so $96.50 is spendable.
- Each stock's target is 2,333 [bps](/docs/help/glossary#basis-point-bps): the 7,000 bps the cash reserve leaves, split three ways and rounded down. That is 23.33% of net equity, or $23.33 here.
- Each stock sits at 0%, more than the drift below its target, so the policy suggests a `BUY` of $23.33 for each of NVDAc, GOOGLc and AAPLc. That leaves $30.01 in USDC, close to the <Fact name="cashReservePct" /> cash reserve.
- It suggests no `SUPPLY` and no `BORROW`: there is no stock to supply yet, nothing is supplied to borrow against, and the 100 USDC already covers the runway.

The test `all cash: buys each token toward 23.33%, capped by spendable cash` in `agent/test/policy.test.ts` pins the runway, the targets, the three buys and the missing `BORROW`. In a larger vault a buy stops at the per-trade cap: with 1,000 USDC, the first `BUY` is <Fact name="mandate.perTradeCapUsd" /> (`caps a buy at perTradeCapUsd`). These are suggestions only. Claude can change them, the vault checks each intent, and the fork run's buys were scripted, not taken from the policy.

## Runway and borrowing

The agent keeps a runway: enough USDC to cover a number of days of its own compute cost. It also keeps back any bill it has not paid yet. The daily cost is the average of the days it has recorded over the previous week. If it has recorded none, it uses a default, <Fact name="dailyComputeUsd" /> a day, which the `MAAT_DEFAULT_DAILY_COMPUTE_USD` setting changes. Cash that the policy spends on stock is whatever is left after the unpaid bill and the runway, so a suggested purchase never eats the runway. This is the policy's rule, not the vault's:

```typescript title="agent/src/policy.ts#L63-L64"
  const runwayNeedUsd6 = BigInt(params.runwayDays) * bills.dailyComputeUsd6;
  let spendable = maxBig(0n, s.usdcBalance - bills.unpaidUsd6 - runwayNeedUsd6);
```

When USDC falls below the runway plus the unpaid bill, the policy suggests a borrow of the shortfall, but only if some stock is supplied as collateral and the credit position can be read:

```typescript title="agent/src/policy.ts#L114-L120"
  // 7. BORROW
  const supplied = weights.reduce((a, w) => a + w.suppliedUsd6, 0n);
  const need = runwayNeedUsd6 + bills.unpaidUsd6;
  if (credit && supplied > 0n && s.usdcBalance < need) {
    const amt = need - s.usdcBalance;
    if (big(amt)) out.push({ action: "BORROW", symbol: "USDC", amountUsd6: amt, why: `USDC ${usd(s.usdcBalance)} below ${params.runwayDays}-day compute runway + bills ${usd(need)}` });
  }
```

The policy never computes a health factor. The vault checks one, through its credit adapter, and it refuses a borrow when the market is closed, when a supplied stock is not allowed, is paused, has no valid, fresh feed answer or has no stable multiplier, when a credit view cannot be read, or when the projected health factor would fall below the floor, <Fact name="mandate.minHealthFactor" /> by default. Inside the de-risk window the target applies instead ([Credit and the health factor](/docs/concepts/credit-and-health-factor)). The agent's preview shows these refusals before it sends.

The policy also suggests a `REPAY` when new revenue arrives while there is debt. It repays up to the debt, from cash beyond the runway and the unpaid bill. The vault counts USDC that reaches it by a plain transfer as revenue. Borrowed USDC and the owner's `fund` deposits are not revenue (`test_borrowedUsdcIsNotRevenue`, `test_fundIsNotCountedAsRevenue`). If the vault is paused, the policy suggests nothing but a repay. Nothing in this repository sends the vault revenue yet, which is why [earning](#planned-earning) is planned.

## Paying its own bill

Each round that reaches the proposal step asks Claude, and each answer has a cost. The agent works out the cost from the token counts in the response, with a price table in `agent/src/llm/pricing.ts`, and adds it to that UTC day's compute total. An answer that cannot be used is still billed for its tokens (`bills a failed Claude call and still settles bills` in `runOnce.test.ts`). What the agent owes is the total of those costs minus what it has paid.

The price table is a few lines of code:

```typescript title="agent/src/llm/pricing.ts#L8-L13"
/** Cents per million tokens (claude-api skill model table, cached 2026-10-06). Cache write = 1.25 x input (5 min TTL). */
const CENTS: Record<string, { input: number; output: number; cacheRead: number; cacheWrite: number }> = {
  "claude-sonnet-5-5": { input: 200, output: 1000, cacheRead: 20, cacheWrite: 250 },
  "claude-opus-5-5": { input: 400, output: 2000, cacheRead: 20, cacheWrite: 500 },
};
const FALLBACK = CENTS["claude-opus-5-5"]!;
```

The figures are cents per million tokens, so the `claude-sonnet-5-5` input rate of 200 means $2.00 per million input tokens. The agent prices each answer under the model name the API reports back, and prices any name that is not in the table at the `claude-opus-5-5` rate ([`MAAT_LOOP_MODEL`](/docs/reference/agent-configuration#maat_loop_model)). It rounds the cost of each call up to the next micro-dollar. The table is fixed in the code and dated 2026-10-06 in its comment. The agent does not read prices from Anthropic, so if Anthropic's prices change, the bill is off until `pricing.ts` is updated. These are the rates the code bills, not a price list.

Once at least <Fact name="billMinUsd" /> is owed, the agent puts a bill payment at the front of the round's queue:

```typescript title="agent/src/loop/runOnce.ts#L248-L252"
    const queue: Proposal[] = [];
    if (unpaid >= BILL_MIN_USD6) {
      const usd = usdc6ToUsd(unpaid);
      queue.push({ action: "PAY_EXPENSE", symbol: "USDC", amountUsd: usd, rationale: `Compute bill: $${usd.toFixed(6)} of Claude API usage unpaid as of UTC day ${day}` });
    }
```

- **Claude does not propose it.** The proposal schema has no `PAY_EXPENSE` action, and the system prompt says bills are settled automatically.
- **It goes to the expense payee, not to Anthropic.** The recipient is the address in the agent's `MAAT_EXPENSE_PAYEE` setting, and the deploy script reads an environment variable of the same name and puts that address on the vault's [payee](/docs/help/glossary#payee) list. The README describes the payee as the wallet that pays for the agent's Claude usage (for Agent #1, the owner's), and the runbook in [Deploy your own vault](/docs/guides/deploy-your-own-vault) sets it to the owner's own wallet. The `Decision` does not carry the payee: read the allowlist with `payees()`, and the recipient from the USDC `Transfer` in the same transaction.
- **The vault limits it.** `PAY_EXPENSE` needs a payee on the allowlist and an amount within the balance and under the per-trade and daily caps, which it shares with trades. It works when the market is closed and it is refused while the vault is paused ([The nine checks](/docs/concepts/nine-checks#other-actions)).
- **It is paid once.** The agent stores a send as pending before it leaves, and the next round checks `usedIntent` first. A lost receipt does not make the agent pay the same bill twice (`does not bill again after a PAY_EXPENSE receipt timed out`).
- **Dry run does not pay.** The cost still accrues, because the agent still calls Claude. The README gives an estimate for that cost, and says that no output from the real Claude API is committed yet. With no sends, the unpaid total only grows. The first round in live mode on the same `MAAT_DATA_DIR` that owes at least <Fact name="billMinUsd" /> is built to pay it. The payment is the unpaid total, or the vault's whole USDC balance when that is smaller, in one `PAY_EXPENSE`; a payment of the whole balance leaves the rest owed. When that amount is above the per-trade cap, the vault refuses it with `SIZE_LIMIT` and the bill stays unpaid ([Switch to live mode and run one round](/docs/guides/run-the-agent#switch-to-live-mode-and-run-one-round)).

## Cadence

The loop is built to run one round at a time: it waits for a round to finish, then sleeps. The sleep depends on whether the round found the market open.

```typescript title="agent/src/loop/scheduler.ts#L4-L9"
export const IN_SESSION_MS = 15 * 60_000;
export const OFF_SESSION_MS = 60 * 60_000;

export function nextDelayMs(r: RunReport | null): number {
  return r?.marketOpen === true ? IN_SESSION_MS : OFF_SESSION_MS;
}
```

That is <Fact name="loopMinutes" /> in session and <Fact name="offSessionMinutes" /> outside. The round reads the market state from the vault's own calendar, under the mandate's session mode ([Market hours](/docs/concepts/market-hours)). A round that could not read the market state, or that threw an error, waits <Fact name="offSessionMinutes" /> for the next one. A round that halted after reading an open market runs again <Fact name="loopMinutes" /> later.

Two limits bound what a round can send. By default the agent sends at most <Fact name="maxTxPerDay" /> transactions in a UTC day and halts the round when it reaches that number. A refusal that is worth recording onchain is sent once per reason per day ([Refusal codes](/docs/concepts/refusal-codes#how-the-agent-reacts)).

## Planned: earning

<Callout type="note" title="Planned">

The first step of the loop is not built. The design has other agents pay <Fact name="x402Price" /> over [x402](/docs/help/glossary#x402) to ask whether a trade is safe right now, and the USDC would reach the vault. Earning from x402 safety checks is planned and not built: no service in this repository takes a payment. The [status page](/docs/get-started/status#planned-not-built) lists it with the other planned work.

</Callout>

Two pieces on the receiving end already exist. The vault's `notifyRevenue` records USDC that arrives and emits `RevenueReceived`, and the policy's repay rule reacts to new revenue. Without a source of revenue, the loop as built would run on the treasury the owner puts in, and the compute bill and the runway would come from that treasury and from debt.
